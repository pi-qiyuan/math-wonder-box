Recursive Geometry

Pythagoras Tree

What is a Pythagoras Tree?

The Pythagoras Tree is a plane fractal constructed from squares. It is named after the Greek mathematician Pythagoras because every triple of touching squares encloses a right triangle, illustrating the Pythagorean theorem.

Construction Principle

The construction begins with a single square. On top of this square, two smaller squares are built, scaled such that their sides form a right-angled triangle with the top side of the base square. This process is then applied recursively to the two new squares.

Mathematical Properties

  • Symmetry: If the two smaller squares are equal in size (forming a 45°-45°-90° triangle), the tree is perfectly symmetric.
  • Asymmetry: By varying the angles of the right triangle, the tree can lean or spiral in various directions, creating organic-looking "wind-swept" forms.
  • Finite Area: For a symmetric tree with 45° angles, the entire structure fits within a finite area of 6×4 times the size of the original square.

Organic Complexity

Despite being made entirely of rigid squares and right angles, the higher iterations of the Pythagoras Tree remarkably resemble natural trees, broccoli, or coral. It demonstrates how simple geometric rules can mimic the complex branching structures found in biology.
