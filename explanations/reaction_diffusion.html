Natural Pattern Formation

Reaction-Diffusion System

What is a Reaction-Diffusion System?

A Reaction-Diffusion system is a mathematical model that explains how complex patterns in nature—like zebra stripes, leopard spots, or coral structures—can emerge from the interaction of simple chemical substances.

The Gray-Scott Model

This simulation uses the Gray-Scott model, which involves two virtual chemicals, A and B. Chemical A is continuously added to the system, while B is consumed in a reaction that creates more of itself (autocatalysis) and then slowly drains away. The resulting patterns depend on the rates of feeding (F) and killing (K).

Turing's Legacy

In 1952, the legendary polymath Alan Turing proposed that these systems could be the basis for morphogenesis—the biological process that causes an organism to develop its shape. He proved that a stable uniform state could become unstable and form periodic patterns through the simple combination of diffusion and reaction.

Computational Art

Because these systems require thousands of calculations per pixel for every frame, they are a perfect showcase for GPU acceleration. The patterns you see are "alive"—they grow, divide, and compete for space in real-time, creating a mesmerizing bridge between mathematics and biology.
