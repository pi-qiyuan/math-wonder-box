The Aizawa Attractor

A Spherical Strange Attractor

What is it?

The Aizawa Attractor is a mathematical system that generates a strange attractor with a beautiful, complex structure. It was discovered by mathematician Langford and later named after Aizawa who popularized its study.

Unlike the butterfly-shaped Lorenz attractor, the Aizawa attractor forms a largely spherical shell with a hollow core and a distinct "stalk" or "tail" extending along the Z-axis. It represents a chaotic system that stays bounded within a specific geometry.

The Equations

The system is defined by these three differential equations:

dx/dt = (z - b)x - dy
dy/dt = dx + (z - b)y
dz/dt = c + az - z³/3 - (x² + y²)(1 + ez) + fzx³

Common parameters: a = 0.95, b = 0.7, c = 0.6, d = 3.5, e = 0.25, f = 0.1.

Geometry of Chaos

The attractor is characterized by its almost perfect symmetry in the X and Y dimensions. Points orbit the Z-axis while oscillating up and down. The term (x² + y²)(1 + ez) acts as a damping force that keeps the trajectory near a spherical surface, while the other terms provide the chaotic energy that prevents the path from ever repeating.

Visualizing the Attraction

In this visualization, we typically project the 3D trajectory onto the X-Z plane (side view) or X-Y plane (top view). The side view reveals the distinct heart-like or spherical profile with its vertical axis of symmetry, while the top view shows the spiral orbits around the center.
