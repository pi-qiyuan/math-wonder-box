Iterated Function System

Barnsley Fern

What is the Barnsley Fern?

The Barnsley Fern is a fractal named after the British mathematician Michael Barnsley who first described it in his book Fractals Everywhere. It is a classic example of an Iterated Function System (IFS) that creates a self-similar structure resembling a natural Black Spleenwort fern.

How is it generated?

The fern is created by repeatedly applying one of four affine transformations to a point, chosen at random based on specific probabilities:

  • Stem (1%): Maps the point to the base of the stem.
  • Leaflets (85%): Creates successively smaller copies of the entire fern.
  • Left Leaflet (7%): Creates the largest left-hand leaflet.
  • Right Leaflet (7%): Creates the largest right-hand leaflet.

Self-Similarity

The beauty of the Barnsley Fern lies in its recursive nature. Every small part of the leaf is a miniature, transformed version of the entire fern. This mathematical model demonstrates how complex biological shapes can emerge from simple recursive rules.
