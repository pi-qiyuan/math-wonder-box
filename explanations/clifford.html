Dynamic Clifford Attractor

Clifford Attractor

What is a Clifford Attractor?

Clifford attractors are a form of strange attractor defined by two simple recursive equations involving trigonometric functions. They were popularized by Clifford Pickover.

xn+1 = sin(a · yn) + c · cos(a · xn)
yn+1 = sin(b · xn) + d · cos(b · yn)

How is it rendered?

Instead of checking if a point escapes (like the Mandelbrot set), we start with a single point and repeatedly apply the equations above millions of times. Each resulting coordinate is plotted on the screen. The beautiful patterns emerge from the density of these points over time.

Strange Beauty

The resulting shapes are "strange attractors" because they exhibit fractal structures and sensitive dependence on the initial parameters (a, b, c, d). Even tiny changes to these numbers can completely transform the resulting pattern from a simple loop into an incredibly complex, feathery structure.
