Recursive Fractals

Sierpinski Carpet

What is a Sierpinski Carpet?

The Sierpinski Carpet is a plane fractal first described by Wacław Sierpiński in 1916. It is a generalization of the Cantor set to two dimensions; another similar generalization is the Sierpinski triangle.

Construction Algorithm

The construction of the Sierpinski carpet begins with a square. The square is cut into 9 congruent sub-squares in a 3-by-3 grid, and the central sub-square is removed. The same procedure is then applied recursively to the remaining 8 sub-squares, ad infinitum.

Mathematical Properties

  • Area: The area of the carpet is zero in the limit of infinite iterations.
  • Fractal Dimension: It has a Hausdorff dimension of approximately 1.8928, meaning its complexity lies between a 1D line and a 2D surface.
  • Self-Similarity: Every part of the carpet, no matter how small, contains a reduced-size copy of the whole.

Infinite Precision

Despite having no area, the carpet has an infinite perimeter. It is a striking example of how simple recursive rules can create shapes that challenge our intuitive understanding of geometry.
