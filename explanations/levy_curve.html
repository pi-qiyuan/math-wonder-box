Self-Similar Fractals

Lévy C Curve

What is the Lévy C Curve?

The Lévy C Curve is a self-similar fractal curve first described by Ernesto Cesàro and later analyzed by Paul Lévy. It is a famous member of the family of "iterated function system" fractals, known for its delicate, feather-like or fabric-like appearance.

How is it constructed?

Start with a straight line segment. In each step, replace the segment with two segments of equal length that meet at a 90-degree angle, forming the two legs of an isosceles right triangle using the original segment as the hypotenuse. Repeating this process creates the characteristic "C" shape that folds into itself with increasing complexity.

Mathematical Properties

  • Fractal Dimension: The Hausdorff dimension of the Lévy C Curve is 2, meaning that as the iterations tend to infinity, it eventually begins to fill a two-dimensional area of the plane.
  • Boundary: The boundary of the Lévy C Curve is itself a fractal, resembling the Koch Snowflake in its complexity.
  • Symmetry: It possesses an inherent 45-degree rotational symmetry in its recursive steps, leading to its balanced, woven look.

Art in Logic

The Lévy C Curve is a favorite among generative artists because of its "woven" texture. It demonstrates how a single, simple geometric rule (folding a line into a right triangle) can lead to structures that look like organic lace or intricate mathematical tapestries.
