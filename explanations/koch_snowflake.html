Fractal Geometry

Koch Snowflake

What is the Koch Snowflake?

The Koch Snowflake is one of the earliest fractal curves to have been described. It first appeared in a 1904 paper by the Swedish mathematician Helge von Koch. It is a famous example of a curve that is continuous everywhere but differentiable nowhere.

How is it constructed?

The construction starts with an equilateral triangle. In each subsequent iteration, every line segment is divided into three equal parts. An equilateral triangle is then built pointing outwards using the middle part as its base, and that base segment is removed. This process is repeated infinitely.

Mathematical Paradoxes

  • Infinite Perimeter: As the iterations tend to infinity, the perimeter of the snowflake increases by a factor of 4/3 at each step, eventually becoming infinite.
  • Finite Area: Despite having an infinite perimeter, the entire snowflake fits within a finite circle. Its area is exactly 8/5 times the area of the original triangle.

Beauty in Logic

The Koch Snowflake captures the essence of fractal geometry: complex, organic-looking structures generated from simple, deterministic rules. It resembles the crystalline structure of real snowflakes, demonstrating how nature uses recursive patterns to create efficient and beautiful forms.
