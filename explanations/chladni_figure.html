Acoustic Resonance Patterns

Chladni Figure

What is a Chladni Figure?

Chladni figures are patterns formed by particles, such as sand or salt, on a vibrating plate or membrane. These patterns reveal the nodal lines of the vibration—areas where the surface is stationary.

z = a cos(nπx) cos(mπy) + b cos(mπx) cos(nπy) = 0

Visualizing Sound

When a plate is bowed or vibrated at a specific resonant frequency, it vibrates in complex ways. Particles move away from the vibrating parts (antinodes) and collect at the stationary parts (nodes), creating intricate geometric designs that vary with the frequency and the shape of the plate.

History

These figures are named after Ernst Chladni, a German physicist and musician, who published his techniques in 1787. His work laid the foundations for the modern science of acoustics and was one of the first ways to "see" sound.
