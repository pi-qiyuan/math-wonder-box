Fibonacci Phyllotaxis

The Mathematics of Nature's Design

What is Phyllotaxis?

Phyllotaxis is the arrangement of leaves on a plant stem, or the pattern of seeds in a sunflower. In many plants, these patterns follow the Fibonacci sequence and the Golden Angle (approximately 137.5 degrees).

The Mathematics

The model used here is based on Vogel's formula, which describes the position of each seed (n) in polar coordinates (r, θ):

r = c √n
θ = n × 137.508°

Where:

  • n is the index number of the seed.
  • r is the distance from the center.
  • θ is the angle from a reference direction.
  • c is a constant scaling factor.

Why the Golden Angle?

The Golden Angle ensures that each new seed is placed as far away as possible from previous ones, leading to the most efficient packing. If the angle were slightly different (e.g., exactly 137.5° or 137.6°), gaps or straight lines would eventually appear as the pattern grows.
