Oscillating Harmonics

Lissajous Curve

What is a Lissajous Curve?

A Lissajous curve (also known as a Lissajous figure or Bowditch curve) is the graph of a system of parametric equations which describe complex harmonic motion.

x(t) = A sin(at + δ)
y(t) = B sin(bt)

Mathematical Harmony

The appearance of the figure is highly sensitive to the ratio a/b. For a ratio of 1, the figure is an ellipse, with special cases including circles (A = B, δ = π/2) and lines (δ = 0). Other simple ratios produce more complex closed curves.

History

These curves were first investigated by Nathaniel Bowditch in 1815 and later in more detail by Jules Antoine Lissajous in 1857. They are often used in physics and electronics to determine the frequency and phase of unknown signals compared to a reference signal.
