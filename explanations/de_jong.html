De Jong Attractor

What is a De Jong Attractor?

The De Jong attractor is a strange attractor discovered by astrophysicist Peter de Jong. Like the Clifford attractor, it is built from a pair of recursive equations combining sine and cosine terms, but the two curves are decoupled in a way that produces its own distinctive family of layered, nebula-like patterns.

xn+1 = sin(a · yn) - cos(b · xn)
yn+1 = sin(c · xn) - cos(d · yn)

How is it rendered?

Starting from a single point, the equations above are applied over and over, hundreds of thousands of times per frame. Each new (x, y) coordinate is plotted as a pixel. No single point tells you much, but as millions of points accumulate, the underlying structure of the attractor emerges from the pattern of their density.

Strange Beauty

With only four parameters (a, b, c, d) driving the whole system, the De Jong attractor can produce an astonishing range of forms — from tightly wound spirals to soft, cloud-like drifts of points reminiscent of a nebula. Small adjustments to any one parameter can reshape the entire pattern, making it one of the most visually rich families of strange attractors to explore.
