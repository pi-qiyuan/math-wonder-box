Epicycloid & Epitrochoid

What is an Epicycloid?

An epicycloid is a plane curve produced by tracing the path of a chosen point on the circumference of a circle (the rolling circle) which rolls without slipping around a fixed circle. If the point lies inside or outside the rolling circle, the curve is called an epitrochoid (often recognized as Spirograph art).

x(θ) = (R + r) cos(θ) - d cos((R + r)θ / r)
y(θ) = (R + r) sin(θ) - d sin((R + r)θ / r)

Parameters & Shapes

In these equations, R is the radius of the fixed center circle, r is the radius of the rolling outer circle, and d is the distance of the tracing pen from the center of the rolling circle. When d = r, the curve is a standard epicycloid such as the Cardioid (R=1, r=1) or the Nephroid (R=2, r=1).

History and Spirograph Art

These curves have been studied by mathematicians since antiquity, including Albrecht Dürer, Ole Rømer, and Isaac Newton. They are the mathematical basis for the famous Spirograph toy, drawing complex mandala-like patterns based on simple ratios of gears.
