Recursive Fractals

Sierpinski Triangle

What is a Sierpinski Triangle?

The Sierpinski Triangle (also known as the Sierpinski Gasket or Sierpinski Sieve) is a fractal and attractive fixed set first described by Wacław Sierpiński in 1915. It is one of the most famous examples of a self-similar set, meaning it is mathematically generated to look the same at any level of magnification.

How it is Created

The construction begins with an equilateral triangle. The triangle is divided into four smaller equilateral triangles by connecting the midpoints of its sides. The central triangle is removed, and the process is repeated recursively for the remaining three triangles.

Mathematical Connections

  • Pascal's Triangle: If you color the odd numbers in Pascal's triangle, the resulting pattern is a Sierpinski triangle.
  • Chaos Game: The triangle can also be generated by a random process called the "Chaos Game," where a point moves halfway toward one of the triangle's vertices selected at random.
  • Fractal Dimension: It has a Hausdorff dimension of log(3)/log(2) ≈ 1.585, which means it is "more than a line but less than a surface."

Infinite Complexity

Like the Sierpinski Carpet, the triangle has an area that approaches zero as iterations approach infinity, while its total perimeter length becomes infinite. It is a beautiful illustration of how simple recursive rules can lead to infinite geometric depth.
