Fibonacci Tree

What is a Fibonacci Tree?

A Fibonacci tree is a fractal structure where each branch splits into two new branches following a recursive pattern. In its most classic form, the lengths of the branches are scaled by the Golden Ratio (φ), reflecting the growth patterns found in real trees and plants.

Branch Scaling ≈ 1/φ and 1/φ²

Mathematical Harmony

The recursion mimics the Fibonacci sequence (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8...), where each number is the sum of the two preceding ones. As the tree grows deeper, the number of branches at each level often relates to these numbers, creating a sense of natural complexity from simple rules.

Biological Inspiration

Many plants use this recursive branching to maximize sunlight exposure or efficiently transport nutrients. By using the Golden Ratio, the tree avoids self-shadowing and creates a dense, balanced canopy. This is a prime example of how nature "calculates" optimal structures through evolution.
