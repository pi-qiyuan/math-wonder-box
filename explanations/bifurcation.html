Chaos Theory

Bifurcation Diagram (Logistic Map)

What is a Bifurcation Diagram?

A bifurcation diagram shows how the stable states of a dynamic system change as a parameter of the system is varied. The most famous example is based on the Logistic Map, a simple mathematical model of population growth.

x[n+1] = r · x[n](1 - x[n])

The Road to Chaos

In this equation, x represents the population ratio and r is a growth parameter. As we increase r, the system's behavior changes dramatically:

  • Stability: For low values of r, the population settles to a single fixed value.
  • Bifurcation: At a critical point, the system "splits" and begins oscillating between two values. This is called a bifurcation.
  • Period Doubling: As r increases further, it splits again into 4, then 8, then 16 values... this is the Feigenbaum cascade.
  • Chaos: Eventually, the system enters a chaotic regime where the values never repeat and are highly sensitive to initial conditions.

Pomeau-Manneville Windows

Even within the chaotic regions, you can see thin vertical strips of white space. These are "windows of order" where the system briefly becomes stable again before returning to chaos. This fractal structure repeats at every scale.

Scientific Impact

The bifurcation diagram proved that extreme complexity and "random" behavior can emerge from very simple, deterministic rules. It is a cornerstone of modern nonlinear dynamics and chaos theory.
