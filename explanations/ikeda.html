Dynamic Ikeda Attractor

Ikeda Attractor

What is an Ikeda Attractor?

The Ikeda attractor comes from the work of physicist Kensuke Ikeda, who modeled how light behaves inside a nonlinear optical resonator — a ring cavity where a laser beam bounces around and interferes with itself. The rotation angle of each bounce depends on the beam's own intensity, which is what gives the system its chaotic character.

tn = c1 - c2 / (1 + xn² + yn²)
xn+1 = 1 + u(xncos tn - ynsin tn)
yn+1 = u(xnsin tn + yncos tn)

How is it rendered?

Starting from a single point, we repeatedly apply the equations above: first computing a phase shift tn based on the point's current distance from the origin, then rotating the point by that angle and scaling it by the damping factor u. Plotting each resulting coordinate, frame after frame, reveals swirling bands where the light-like trajectories settle.

Strange Beauty

Because the rotation angle itself depends on position, points that start out close together can spiral apart in wildly different directions — a textbook example of sensitive dependence on initial conditions. The damping parameter u acts like a dial: turn it and the pattern shifts between tight, storm-like vortices and looser, wing-shaped sweeps, all traced out by a single point in deterministic motion.
