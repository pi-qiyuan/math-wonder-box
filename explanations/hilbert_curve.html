Space-Filling Curves

Hilbert Curve

What is a Hilbert Curve?

The Hilbert Curve is a continuous fractal space-filling curve first described by the German mathematician David Hilbert in 1891. It is a variant of the Peano curves and has the remarkable property of being able to fill a two-dimensional square using a one-dimensional line without gaps or self-intersections.

How it works

The curve is generated recursively. At each level of recursion (or "order"), the square is divided into four quadrants, and a lower-order curve is placed in each, rotated and connected to form a single continuous path. As the order increases, the curve becomes increasingly dense, eventually visiting every point in the square as the order approaches infinity.

Mathematical Significance

  • Locality Preservation: Points that are close together on the one-dimensional line are generally close together in the two-dimensional space. This makes it useful in computer science for tasks like image processing and multidimensional indexing.
  • Fractal Dimension: The Hilbert curve has a Hausdorff dimension of 2, meaning it effectively behaves like a 2D surface despite being a 1D line.

Beauty in Order

Visually, the Hilbert curve appears as an incredibly intricate and regular maze. Its perfectly square structure and recursive symmetry make it one of the most elegant examples of mathematical order.
